Two traffic-light lessons, compared

You gave Codex and me the same brief: 20261019_exercise.md, byte-for-byte identical in both directories. It asks for a breadboard/ site whose first lesson is the 4017 + OR-gate traffic light, in 15 sections, in English and Russian. This page reviews everything in lssk_codex/breadboard and compares it with my breadboard/m11_traffic_light.

I wrote one of the two sites. To keep this fair:
  • I ran Codex's own scripts myself, on copies: Icarus 12, Icarus 14, Verilator 5.053, licensed Questa 2025.2 and Yosys 0.69.
  • I put the same 12 bugs into both designs.
  • I list my own weak points next to Codex's.

Codex was still editing while I worked. Its README, pages and Questa notes changed at 13:21–13:22; its HDL has not changed since 07:30. This review is of a snapshot taken at 13:27 PDT on 21 October. I changed nothing in lssk_codex.

The rules were not the same for both of us. Codex got yuri_rules_3.txt, with eight rules. I got yuri_rules_4.txt, which has two of them plus "Asynchronous reset is also OK". The style section checks both sites against all of the rules.

Verdict

  • Both sites are complete and work. Each has all 15 sections, English and Russian, three schematics, two equivalent designs, chip models, scripts for three simulators, and BGM and Tiny Tapeout wrappers. Codex's site is polished, carefully cross-checked and honest about what it did not test.
  • Codex's verification is broader, and that is the main thing to take from it. It has a unit test for the chip models (all 256 input patterns of the OR chips, plus the 4017's CARRY and CLOCK INHIBIT) and a bench that simulates all three wrappers with both designs. Those benches catch three bugs that mine misses.
  • The biggest technical problem is timeunit 1ns; timeprecision 1ps; in every synthesizable file. Yosys's standard reader rejects them in the newest version here (0.69), not only in the "older 0.43" that Codex blames. BGM's Yosys flow and a Tiny Tapeout build would fail unless switched to Slang. BGM itself uses timeunit only in testbenches (16 files, all of them tb.sv).
  • The testbenches keep the letter of rules_3 but not the BGM examples. They sample at the edge plus # 1 and drive inputs with blocking assignments in the middle of the cycle. That pattern then needs 19 # 1 delays and two negedge waits.
    • The negedge waits are not needed. Without them, Codex's main bench stops catching a design that counts on the falling edge, but a BGM-style rising-edge checker catches it with no negedge.
    • Codex wrote that no deviation was required and did not write need_to_deviate_from_style.html.
  • Schematic 1 does not show what the brief asked for. The brief asked for flip-flops and gates; Codex draws the state register as a black box.
  • Process notes leak into the student pages. Examples: the typo in "the prompt", the license failure inside Codex's sandbox, a page fetch that timed out. The pages also over-credit Timofei, and the footers say "Traffic-light idea: Timofei Nikiforov".
  • My own gaps, all things Codex has:
    • no unit test for the chip models;
    • wrappers never simulated;
    • no synthesis script;
    • no time unit in tb.sv;
    • bare wire declarations;
    • no CSV or ZIP downloads.

Questionable design choices

  • The generic design is a one-hot ring of ten flip-flops. The ring is a valid RTL idiom. But the 4017 has five flip-flops inside, a Johnson counter, so the lesson misses the chance to show what is in the chip. The ring also has no self-correction. Codex's 4017 model is a binary counter, so the three encodings in the lesson are all different (one-hot RTL, binary model, Johnson chip).
  • Benches stop at the first mismatch with $fatal, print no per-cycle trace and have no final summary. A student sees either one PASS line or one error message. The BGM examples count errors and summarize them in final; mine also prints a table, one line per cycle.
  • Lots of disclaimers in student text. Examples: "No physical circuit has been tested", "not the HDL simulator or an electrical model", "Parts descriptions alone do not establish module electrical specifications". They are honest, but they belong in the README, not in a lesson for 15-year-olds.
  • The ZIP (2.2 MB) ships research/: 33 logs, JSON records and a copy of your rules file.
  • The TT outputs are gated with ena. This is harmless but unnecessary: Tiny Tapeout's docs say ena can be ignored.
  • Two wrapper parameters, CHIP_MODEL and USE_CHIP_MODEL, each select the design with a generate if. This is fine, but the two wrappers name the parameter differently.

Presentation and wording

What works well in Codex's site

  • Clean layout: a dark header with three numbers (4/2/4, 3 chips, 10 states), a sticky table of contents, and each section in its own card. A print stylesheet is included.
  • Every figure is an SVG file with SVG and PNG download links and alt text.
  • Both pages set lang and hreflang, and the two languages link to each other.
  • Seven clean drawings. The state graph has the Moore outputs inside the nodes, and a second graph shows the one-hot codes. The pinouts are clear.
  • The parts table links each part to its exact sheet row, for example range=LSSK!A37:Q37, and comes with CSV files for parts and jumpers.
  • A small JavaScript stepper (Run 1 Hz, Step, Reset) shows the ten states and the lamps. It works offline.
  • A troubleshooting table and "try next" tasks. One useful note: the source's "outputs 1–4, 5–6, 7–10" counts from one, while the datasheet says Q0–Q9.

What doesn't

  • Notes meant for you appear on student pages:
    • "the prompt's 'hackaton_top' omits an h", which also appears as a comment inside hackathon_top.sv;
    • "The live Google sheet was unavailable during this update";
    • "Icarus 13 … is not installed here";
    • the FlexNet host-ID story;
    • "fetch timed out; no technical claims rely on it";
    • "the quotation in the assignment and archived text were used".
  • The breadboard drawing is hard to follow. Ten signal wires leave the board and loop around its left side. Labels cover row numbers and pin numbers, and the clock and reset modules are not drawn. A student needs the hole-by-hole table to build it, and that table is collapsed.
  • The timing diagram is drawn by hand, not taken from the simulation. It has no reset trace. The clock starts high at t = 0, so the first counting edge is ambiguous.
  • Code has no syntax highlighting and is collapsed. There is no dark mode.
  • Calling the Habr article "Yuri Panchul's course" and listing it under "Course and the traffic-light exercise" is vague. The lesson number, 1.7, would help readers find it.

Russian page

The translation is complete and matches the English section by section. The problems are terminology and a few calques:

  • «микросхемы CMOS» throughout; the page never uses «КМОП».
  • «Сброс асинхронный, активный высокий» is a calque of "active high". A few lines later the page says «активен единицей», so the wording is also inconsistent.
  • «Двухвходовый ИЛИ даёт 1» is missing a noun: «двухвходовый элемент ИЛИ».
  • «даташит» is jargon in a school text. Use «техническое описание (datasheet)».
  • «Сброс выбирает S0» is a calque. Use «сброс переводит автомат в S0».
  • «выемкой вверх» is understandable, but «ключом вверх» is the usual term for the notch on a DIP package.

Ideas to adopt

From Codex into my lesson

I have not changed anything yet. Tell me which of these to do.

  1. A unit-test bench for the chip models, in the posedge style: every OR input pattern on every pin, plus the 4017's CARRY and CLOCK INHIBIT. CLOCK INHIBIT can be tested by driving CLOCK as data from the bench's clock. This closes the three bugs I miss.
  2. A simulation of the wrappers (lab_top, hackathon_top, project.v) with both designs. I have never simulated mine.
  3. A synthesis check script: Yosys read_verilog -sv; synth; check -assert, run on the stock reader.
  4. Parts linked to their sheet rows, while keeping my prices.
  5. CSV downloads for parts and wires, and a ZIP of the exercise without build logs.
  6. The note about counting from one ("outputs 1–4" are Q0–Q3).
  7. A troubleshooting table that maps each symptom to what to check.
  8. rules_3 items, if you want them in my code too:
    • timeunit 1ns; timeprecision 1ps; in tb.sv only;
    • logic for DUT outputs and unused nets;
    • a watchdog counted in clock cycles.
  9. Optional: the small state stepper widget.

From my lesson into Codex's

  1. Schematic 1 with real flip-flops and gates. The Johnson counter and decoder are what is inside the 4017.
  2. A breadboard drawing that looks like the board, with the modules on it, plus a checklist with tick boxes.
  3. A waveform made from the simulation's VCD, so the picture and the testbench cannot disagree.
  4. A rising-edge checker with was_reset and final, which removes all # 1 delays and negedge waits.
  5. RTL that the stock Yosys reader accepts, so BGM's Yosys flow and Tiny Tapeout work without Slang.
  6. The kit's traffic-light module and prices from the sheet.
  7. A clean -Wall run, syntax highlighting, copy buttons and dark mode.
