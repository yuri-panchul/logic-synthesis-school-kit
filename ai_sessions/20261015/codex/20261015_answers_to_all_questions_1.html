9. Why five buttons and an ARM/TRANSFER protocol for the three-FIFO design?

The old version actually allocated five pulse controls—PUSH_A, PUSH_B, ARM, TRANSFER and POP_C—plus RESET, making six button modules before purchasing-pack rounding. That was a different protocol I invented, not the one in your RTL.

The revised kit has three operational buttons: A_VALID, B_VALID and SUM_READY. Operand data and reset use rail jumpers. The internal transaction is automatic:

join = !empty_A & !empty_B & !full_SUM
pop_A = pop_B = push_SUM = join
sum_input = head_A + head_B

This follows your named module, particularly lines 74–79, together with its valid/ready wrapper. SUM_READY controls consuming the output FIFO; it does not manually trigger the internal addition. The FIFO kit documentation contains no ARM/TRANSFER operation.

The source defaults to width=8 and depth=10. The single-package breadboard queues are 4 bits × 16 words, so the comparison uses width=4 and depth=16. The queue protocol is retained; the capacity/width difference is explicit. Pulse-level conversion to the physical 40105 remains unverified and is isolated in the uncertainty file.
