One queue, six visible signals

This is the separate kit requested in the October 15 review: one FIFO, an assembled clock generator, PUSH and POP buttons, four self-locking touch switches for input data, and LEDs for the four output bits plus EMPTY and FULL.

The kit allocates exactly two button modules. The supplier’s five-module lot is a purchasing unit, not five controls in the experiment. The four TTP223 modules are the specific user-requested part; they are additional to the original LSSK tab. Their $0.25/module price is a budgeting assumption because the listing price could not be retrieved. The four modules contribute $1.00 to this estimate.

Use ready-assembled modules with header pins fitted and toggle mode already configured. At the chip level, TTP223 supports direct/toggle operation and output-polarity options; the board’s jumper labels must be checked against the supplied variant. The kit does not ask the student to assemble a touch-sensor circuit. Tontek mode-selection reference.

Connection budget

One CD4081 provides four AND gates, budgeted for request/ready qualification and clock-command routing. One CD4069 provides the two status inversions, the negative-edge pop polarity and spare inverters. The working estimate is 40 M–M leads and 30 M–F leads: 12 for four touch modules, six for two buttons, nine for the LED bar and three for the clock. Kit jumper quantities include the stated 25% reserve and pack rounding.

The CD74HC40105E pin map is VCC=16, GND=8, MR=9, /OE=1, DIR=2, SI=3, D0…D3=4…7, Q0…Q3=13,12,11,10, DOR=14 and SO=15. Tie /OE low for visible outputs. Clock-command routing must respect the FIFO’s pulse interface; the confirmed edge/flag facts and unverified adapter details are documented separately. The Boolean gate budget is not a bench-tested netlist.

Try it

  1. Initialize with PUSH and POP released. EMPTY should be on and FULL off.
  2. Set 0011, request one write cycle, then release PUSH. Change the switches to 1010 and request another write cycle.
  3. Observe 0011 at the output. Request one read cycle; the next word becomes 1010.
  4. Read the second word. EMPTY becomes active; the data LEDs have no valid-word meaning while empty.
  5. Keep requesting writes until all sixteen word positions are occupied. FULL becomes active.
  6. Request a read, then a later write. Space becomes available and the queue can accept another word.

Holding PUSH or POP across several eligible clock cycles requests several operations. The buttons express requests; the assembled module supplies the clock.
