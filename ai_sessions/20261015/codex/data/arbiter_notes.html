Four logic ICs, one bit of scheduling history

Let p=0 prefer requester 0 and p=1 prefer requester 1 when both request. Each rising edge of the assembled clock accepts the visible grant. There is no separate downstream ready signal in this exercise.

g0 = r0 AND (NOT r1 OR NOT p)
g1 = r1 AND (NOT r0 OR p)
p_next = g0 OR (p AND NOT (r0 OR r1))
RESET: p = 0

When both request, accepting g0 makes p=1; accepting g1 makes p=0. With no requests, priority is retained. With one request, that request wins regardless of priority. These are combinational grants for the upcoming sampling edge; after the edge, the displayed grants may immediately change to the next decision.

Gate allocation: CD4011 supplies three inversions by joining each NAND’s two inputs (¬r0, ¬r1, ¬(r0∨r1)). CD4071 supplies four OR gates (r0∨r1, the two grant conditions, and the next-state merge). CD4081 supplies three AND gates (g0, g1, and idle hold). One half of CD4013 stores p and supplies both p and ¬p. Thus the advertised four core packages are sufficient.

Connect p_next to 4013 D, the assembled 555 module OUT directly to CLK, RESET to its active-high reset, and SET to GND. Buttons are r0, r1 and RESET. Set the clock near 0.5–1 Hz and change requests between rising edges.

Check the behavior

  1. Reset: p=0. Both requests low must give no grant.
  2. Exercise each single request with both possible p values.
  3. Hold both requests high for eight clock cycles. Record the grant just before each rising edge: 0,1,0,1,0,1,0,1.
  4. Insert three idle clock cycles; confirm that the next contested decision retains the saved priority.
  5. For every cycle check NOT(g0 AND g1), g0 → r0 and g1 → r1.

The accompanying software checks exhaust all input/state combinations and persistent-request traces. They verify these equations; physical operation still requires the pilot build.
