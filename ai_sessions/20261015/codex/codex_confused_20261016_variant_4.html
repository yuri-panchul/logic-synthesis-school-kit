1. Original mistaken statement by Codex / Первоначальное ошибочное утверждение Codex

English

Published under the heading “Issues Codex is confident about”, in the section “The automatic adder must also check result-queue capacity”:

In your reference module, lines 74–79 combine both operand-valid signals with the result queue’s readiness. The wrapper, lines 28–34, turns valid/ready into push/pop. Therefore pop_A = pop_B = push_SUM = !empty_A & !empty_B & !full_SUM. If A/B were consumed while SUM was full, those operands would be lost without storing their result. This is a definite functional error in such an implementation, not evidence of a need for analog support components.

Source: original English report, 15 October 2026.

3. Assistant’s clarification / Разъяснение ассистента

English — response reproduced

No—your implementation correctly handles this condition. My report’s wording was misleading, and I’m sorry. I described a hypothetical error under an “Issues” heading, although your RTL already prevents it.

In your module, lines 74–79, together with the FIFO wrappers:

pop_A = pop_B = push_SUM
      = a_down_valid & b_down_valid & sum_up_ready

Therefore:

  • Both operands are removed together, exactly when their sum is accepted.
  • When the result FIFO is full, sum_up_ready = 0, so neither operand is removed.

Your testbench passes because this behavior is implemented correctly. It independently queues accepted A and B values, compares each accepted result against their oldest pair, and checks for leftover operands and mismatched transfer counts. It exercises independent inputs, backpressure, and randomized traffic.

I ran your unchanged RTL and testbench with Icarus 12, adding an observation-only monitor:

  • 100 results in 261 cycles, with no reported errors.
  • 54 cycles with both operands available but the result FIFO full.
  • No operand consumption during those blocked cycles; all three internal transfers remained synchronized.

The report should have said: “Your RTL already implements the required output-capacity check. The proposed breadboard implementation must preserve that behavior.”
