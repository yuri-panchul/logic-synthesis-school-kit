Build a FIFO adder

Two numbers arrive at different times, and the circuit adds them anyway. FIFO A and FIFO B hold the operands in the order they came, a 4008 adds the pair at their heads, and FIFO C collects the sums. When nobody takes sums out of C, it fills up and A and B wait: that is backpressure, the flow control used in every processor and network chip.

{{stats}}
Before you start, do the getting-started steps and, if you can, the round-robin arbiter: this build uses the same breadboard conventions, the same modules and the same 4013 flip-flop. It is a group project for two or three students. You build it in two stages and test after each: stage 1 is the 40105 FIFO demo with one queue, stage 2 turns it into the adder.

How it works

The FIFO. A 40105 is a queue of 16 words, 4 bits each. A rising edge on SI (shift in) takes the word on D0–D3, if DIR (data-in ready) says there is room. The word ripples to the output end in about a microsecond; DOR (data-out ready) then says that a word sits on Q0–Q3. A falling edge on SO (shift out) drops that word, and the next one moves up. MR clears the queue. The operands here are 3 bits wide, with D3 tied to 0, so the largest sum, 7 + 7 = 14, still fits into the 4 bits of FIFO C.

Buttons in step with the clock. A button bounces: one press makes several edges within a few milliseconds, and every rising edge on SI would load another copy of the number. So each of the push A, push B and pop C buttons goes into the D input of a flip-flop. The flip-flop looks at the button only at the rising edge of the clock, so one press gives one clean edge. Hold a button until its LED reacts, about half a second.

The transfer. A pair can move when A has data (DOR of A, rA), B has data (rB) and C has room (DIR of C, fC). A 4-input AND computes go, and a flip-flop turns it into the transfer pulse t:

go = rA AND rB AND fC AND NOT t
t takes the value of go at every rising clock edge
t rises: SI of C rises, C takes A + B
t falls one clock later: SO of A and B fall, A and B drop the pair

C takes the sum first and A and B let go of the pair a whole clock later, so the sum is stable when C stores it. The NOT t input brings t back to 0 after one tick, so every transfer is one clean pulse and a pair moves at most every second tick. Why not simply clock AND ready? The Habr article suggests a pulse like shift_in = clock AND push. For the transfer that would be SI of C = SO of A and B = clk AND rA AND rB AND fC. But the 40105 answers within a microsecond, while the clock is still high: DIR of C drops for a moment after every load, and DOR of A and B drop and rise again with the next word. The pulse ends early, the pair is popped, the flags come back and the pulse starts again. In a simulation of that circuit, all the waiting pairs moved within a microsecond in one clock tick, and C caught sums before the 4008 had finished adding. The flip-flop keeps the decision fixed for a whole clock period. Chip designers follow the same rule: state changes only at clock edges.

  2. Rails, 4 wires. The bottom board gets its power from the top one: wires 1–4 in the checklist, in columns {{rail_cols}} at the right end.
  3. Chips. On the bottom board, notch on the left: 4013 #1 in columns {{cols_U1}} (pin 1 in {{p1_U1}}) and 40105 A in columns {{cols_A}} (pin 1 in {{p1_A}}).

  5. Power, tie-offs and the LED module ground, as listed in stage 1 of the checklist. Then connect the 9 V cable for a moment: the power module LED lights and no chip gets warm. Disconnect it again.

Each module: + to the bottom + rail of the bottom board, − to the bottom − rail, OUT to its column on the bottom board: pop C to {{mod_popC}}, clock to {{mod_clk}}, reset to {{mod_rst}}, push A to {{mod_pushA}}, data d0, d1 and d2 to {{mod_d0}}, {{mod_d1}} and {{mod_d2}}. Turn the potentiometer of the 555 module to about 2 ticks per second.

Test it. LED 1 of the A/B module shows DOR of A, "A has data"; LEDs 2, 3 and 4 show the word at the head of A, a2 a1 a0.

Test the adder

On the module on C, LED 3 shows DOR, "C has data", and LEDs 4–7 show Q0–Q3, so the sum reads from right to left: LED 7 is the top bit. LED 1 is always on (it sits on VCC), LED 2 goes off while you pop and LED 8 lights while you hold reset. The yellow LED is "C has room".

If something does not work

  • Nothing lights on the bottom board: it gets power only through the four rail wires 1–4. Check them, and the power module as in the getting-started steps.
  • One press puts a number in twice: the push button reaches SI directly instead of through its flip-flop. SI of A must come from pin 1 of 4013 #1 (sA), SI of B from pin 1 of 4013 #2 (sB).
  • Nothing goes into a FIFO: check the clock (the LED on the 555 module blinks), MR on the reset wire and the reset button, which must give 0 when it is not pressed.
  • Pairs never move into C: check the 4082: rA on pin 5, rB on pin 4, fC on pin 3, NOT t on pin 2 (from pin 12 of 4013 #2) and go from pin 1 to pin 9 of 4013 #2; t goes from pin 13 of 4013 #2 to SI of C and SO of A and B.
  • Sums are wrong: check the operand wires to the 4008 (a0, a1, a2 on pins 7, 5, 3; b0, b1, b2 on pins 6, 4, 2) and its tie-offs: A4 (pin 1), B4 (pin 15) and CIN (pin 9) to GND.
  • C shows a number but LED 3 is off: that is normal. An empty 40105 keeps showing its last word; only DOR says whether it is valid.
  • A chip gets hot: it is in backwards. Unplug the power at once.
  • Find a fault with a probe: a single 5V LED with its long leg in a column and its short leg in the − rail shows the signal there; compare it with the tick table above. On the outputs of the 4013s the probe can pull the signal down, so take it out again after a look.

Next: the same adder in SystemVerilog

In SystemVerilog the FIFOs are synchronous: push and pop are sampled at the clock edge, together with everything else. So the transfer condition can go straight to push and pop, and a pair moves on every clock. The 40105 reacts to edges on SI and SO at any moment, which is why the breadboard needs the transfer flip-flop and moves a pair every second tick.

module fifo_adder
(
    input              clk,
    input              rst,

    input        [2:0] in_data,
    input              push_a,
    input              push_b,
    input              pop_c,

    output       [3:0] c_head,
    output             c_empty
);

    logic [2:0] a_head, b_head;
    logic       a_empty, b_empty, c_full;

    wire        move = ~ a_empty & ~ b_empty & ~ c_full;
    wire  [3:0] sum  = a_head + b_head;

    // sync_fifo: any synchronous FIFO with push, pop, empty and full
    sync_fifo # (.width (3), .depth (16)) fifo_a (.clk, .rst,
        .push (push_a), .write_data (in_data), .pop (move),
        .read_data (a_head), .empty (a_empty), .full ());

    sync_fifo # (.width (3), .depth (16)) fifo_b (.clk, .rst,
        .push (push_b), .write_data (in_data), .pop (move),
        .read_data (b_head), .empty (b_empty), .full ());

    sync_fifo # (.width (4), .depth (16)) fifo_c (.clk, .rst,
        .push (move), .write_data (sum), .pop (pop_c),
        .read_data (c_head), .empty (c_empty), .full (c_full));

endmodule
