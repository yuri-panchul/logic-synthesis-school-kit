The catalogue merges duplicates and separates distinct named circuit variants. It contains 61 entries. The custom sequence task is bounded to patterns of 2–4 bits so the stated logic inventory covers every generated task. Narrative mentions of a whole breadboard CPU do not define a fixed exercise architecture or BOM; FPGA/CPU/ASIC sections beyond Habr 1.7 are outside this breadboard inventory.

Quantities and purchasing units

Each exercise lists a working inventory for one learner or pair. A kit reuses parts between exercises and takes the maximum required quantity of each item, not the sum over lessons. Jumper counts include chip supply connections, unused-input ties, fixed data patches and signal wiring. They are layout estimates, not measured counts from an assembled board.

M–F counts use three leads per button or touch module, nine per eight-LED bar, three for the clock module and four for the traffic-light module. M–M estimates are specified individually for each exercise. Kits add 25% to both jumper types and round up to 40-lead packs. Button modules are allocated individually from the supplier’s five-module lots: three controls mean three modules in the kit. CSV packs_to_buy describes supplier lots for a one-kit order; line costs describe the allocated components in a kit assembled from bulk stock. Unused units from a source lot are available for other kits, not extra student controls.

The Basics inventory covers the listed introductory exercises, including the three simultaneous displays explicitly present in the July list. Three boards are a layout allowance, not a proven physical minimum. Comprehensive covers all catalogue entries, including the source’s rare register file, ALU and optional hexadecimal decoder. Exercises are assembled sequentially; simultaneous classroom stations need separate kits.

Price model

All amounts are USD. Prices for LSSK parts come from column J of the October 14 snapshot. Rows 40 and 41 duplicate CD4048; the model uses row 41 once. Rows 42 and 43 are included for the named rare-chip exercises even though their source quantities are zero. Button row 66 is normalized as $2.03 for five modules; jumper rows 70/71 as $1.30 for forty leads. Supplier variants, stock and current checkout prices are not asserted.

The only newly requested part outside the tab is the TTP223 assembled self-locking touch module. Its $0.25/unit is explicitly an unverified budget assumption, not a retrieved supplier quote. Four are included in the single-FIFO kit and Comprehensive. The chip family’s mode options are documented by Tontek.

CSV record types distinguish component, allowance and summary; summing all numeric rows double-counts totals. Each component line rounds to cents before summation. Loaded cost adds 15% inbound allowance, 5% replacement allowance, packaging and estimated labor at $20/hour. Suggested retail targets 40% contribution after a 3% payment fee and rounds up to $5. Shared classroom USB power/meters, customer delivery, tax and fixed business overhead are excluded. All commercial percentages and labor minutes are planning assumptions.
