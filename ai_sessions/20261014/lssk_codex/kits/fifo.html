A concrete FIFO lesson

First build C11. With SI low and SO low, pulse MR high, then low. Hold a four-bit word stable and pulse SI high then low while DIR is high. After DOR becomes high, the oldest word is valid on Q. A complete SO high→low pulse removes that word on its falling edge. Record input and output lists to prove ordering.

Three queues: freeze permission, capture, then consume

The source proposes the three-FIFO architecture without a completed controller. This kit budgets a manual teaching controller, not a proven automatic streaming controller. The selected FIFO is CD74HC40105E (LSSK row 36), whose shift-out behavior differs in one respect from older CD40105B. Do not mix variants without reviewing their datasheets.

ready = DOR_A AND DOR_B AND DIR_C
permit <= ready on the rising edge of debounced ARM (4013)
transfer = debounced TRANSFER AND permit
SI_C = transfer
SO_A = transfer
SO_B = transfer
SI_A = debounced PUSH_A
SI_B = debounced PUSH_B
SO_C = debounced POP_C
MR_A = MR_B = MR_C = RESET; 4013 RESET = RESET
4013 SET = 0; all three FIFO /OE inputs = 0
4008 carry-in = 0

Two AND gates form ready, and one qualifies TRANSFER. A CD4013 stores permit. The second 4081 in the inventory is a control-expansion allowance. Five buttons—PUSH_A, PUSH_B, ARM, TRANSFER and POP_C—are independently debounced; the sixth is RESET. Four data pins of each input FIFO are set with rail patch leads. Unused 4013 and gate inputs are tied to defined levels.

  1. Reset with every pulse button released. Leave SI and SO low; wait for flags to settle. Load A and B independently, accepting new words only when the respective DIR is high.
  2. When DOR_A, DOR_B and DIR_C are high, press and release ARM. Observe permit=1. Do not modify any FIFO between ARM and TRANSFER.
  3. Press TRANSFER: its rising edge stores the stable low four sum bits in C. Hold it until the conditioned pulse is established. The heads of A and B are still unchanged.
  4. Release TRANSFER: its falling edge removes the consumed words from A and B. Wait for their DOR flags to settle. Press ARM afresh before every subsequent TRANSFER. Never reuse a stale permit.
  5. Consume C independently with a complete POP_C pulse only when DOR_C is high. Observe the output word before the falling edge.

A single live expression clock AND DOR_A AND DOR_B AND DIR_C is unsuitable here: writing C can change DIR_C while the pulse is active. Latching permission first avoids that feedback during the transaction. This manual protocol relies on the stated operator sequence; it does not enforce atomicity under arbitrary button presses. Automatic operation would need an additional state machine and a revised BOM.

Pin reference for the chosen FIFO

CD74HC40105E: VCC=16, GND=8, MR=9, /OE=1, DIR=2, SI=3, D0…D3=4…7, Q0…Q3=13,12,11,10, DOR=14, SO=15. Place one 100 nF capacitor per IC. MR clears validity flags, not stored data bits: ignore Q while DOR=0. TI datasheet, loading/unloading and master reset.

Acceptance sequence for the pilot build

  • Load A=[1,2,3], then B=[4,5,6] at different times. Three transfers must enqueue C=[5,7,9] in order.
  • Leave B empty and press ARM: permit must remain 0 and TRANSFER must not enqueue C.
  • Fill C with 16 results while never popping it. After flags settle, DIR_C must be 0. Fresh ARM must block another transfer and leave the next A/B pair intact.
  • Pop one result, wait for DIR_C, then re-arm and transfer. The waiting pair must be consumed once.
  • Verify exactly one SI rising edge and SO falling edge per physical press/release with the actual switch batch. Repeat after reset and when queues are empty/full.

The three LED bars provide 24 observation channels: 12 data bits, six ready flags, carry and permit, with four channels spare. The FIFO word is four bits: carry is visible during computation but is not stored in C. Restrict sums to 0–15 or explicitly teach modulo-16 arithmetic. A five-bit result queue would require width expansion and a new inventory.
