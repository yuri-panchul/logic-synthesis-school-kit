Corrections and deliberate choices

  • 4077: the source text and spreadsheet describe three-input XNOR gates. The manufacturer identifies four two-input XNOR gates. G13 uses two inputs.
  • 4019: four data paths share Ka and Kb. It can combine two vectors under shared enables, but not select each bit with an independent four-bit mask. M05 explains both meanings and budgets the basic-gate circuit for the latter.
  • 4086: one package supplies one four-wide AOI function. M02 and M04 budget two packages, with the necessary output combining or inversion.
  • FIFO: the project requires three chips, although LSSK G36 contains one and the FIFO quantity column omits them. The chosen catalog part is the linked CD74HC40105E, not an unspecified interchangeable “40105”.
  • Additional IC counts: two 4008s are needed for eight-bit addition and two 4063s for eight-bit comparison. Quantities are derived from the exercises, not copied from G/H/I.
  • Rare devices: CD40108 and CD40181 are required by named exercises despite their zero source quantities. Both are included and visibly flagged as procurement risks. CD4032 and DM9368 also require supplier verification.
  • 4048: rows 40 and 41 duplicate the part. The estimate uses row 41 ($0.55) once, not both rows.
  • 4511 and displays: the July adder/serial-adder exercises specify three simultaneous displays. Each 4511 needs seven separate 560 Ω resistors. A resistor solely in the common cathode does not give independent segment current. The Habr wiring sentence that returns 4511 outputs to 4511 inputs is interpreted as a typo: outputs drive display segments.
  • BCD vs hexadecimal: 4511 represents 0–9, not a general four-bit hexadecimal number or the decimal tens of an addition. O01 separately budgets three DM9368s and their interface; it is not silently substituted into the other exercises.
  • Button pack: row 66 is treated as a five-pack of separate resistor-equipped modules, $2.03 / 5 = $0.406 each. This interpretation and active-high, independent digital outputs must be confirmed against the ordered variant. Rows 65 and 67–69 are not assumed equivalent.
  • Support parts: HCT LED buffers, clock conditioning, debounce and decoupling are additions, with unquoted allowance prices. They are not represented as spreadsheet items.

Common connection standard

Power and packaging

Use breadboard-compatible DIP parts, not surface-mount parts with similar names. Read the actual manufacturer pinout. Standard builds use one USB-to-9 V boost cable (row 57) feeding one regulator module (row 56), set to 5 V, with common ground across all boards. Do not parallel two supply outputs. Each board gets a 10 µF bulk capacitor and each active IC gets a 100 nF local capacitor. Keep logic inputs defined; leave unused outputs open. Rails must stay within 4.5–5.5 V because HCT parts share the supply.

The high-current legacy DM9368 demonstration O01 instead uses the sheet’s direct 5 V path (rows 58/59), avoiding the 9-to-5 V regulator’s heat dissipation. It needs a regulated classroom USB source rated at least 1 A, compatible center-positive barrel wiring, and a checked supply-current budget. Never attach the 9 V cable to this direct breakout. Comprehensive includes both power options for separate exercises.

Observing CMOS outputs

Use one SN74HCT244N per eight logic outputs being observed, with /OE pins low. A 2.2 kΩ series resistor follows each used output, ahead of the already current-limited LED/module. At 5 V this bounds buffer current below 2.3 mA per channel even before LED drop. The resulting brightness must be checked with the actual LED color/batch. Logic nets connect to the buffer inputs, not directly to high-current indicators. Seven-segment LEDs driven by CD4511 have their own 560 Ω segment resistors instead.

The LSSK photos’ no-added-resistor LED arrangement may work for a selected low-current batch, but the source links do not establish its load current. The proposed observation stage avoids relying on CD4000’s relatively weak output drive. Kit costing includes it explicitly. The beginner guides give the complete pin map.

Clock and button conditioning

A 555 module is supplied already assembled. Feed its output through two SN74HCT14 inverters to preserve polarity and provide full CMOS levels. For manual clock, FIFO command or FSM SAMPLE, use: active-high button OUT → 100 kΩ → node; 1 µF from node to GND; node → first HCT14 input → second HCT14 → conditioned signal. A two-inverter channel uses four signal pins; follow the package pinout. Allow at least 0.5 seconds each for press and release at classroom speed, then validate one edge per action on the real switches. RC values are a starting design, not a measured debounce guarantee.

Pull resistors inside buttons do not debounce them. Reset buttons do not need to make a counted pulse, but release reset away from a sampling edge. Data buttons are held stable around SAMPLE. The shared classroom setup should have access to an oscilloscope or logic analyzer for the teacher’s timing qualification; neither is in a student kit. Clock and manual STEP outputs are alternatives selected while power is off.

Special devices

CD40174 reset is active low, unlike CD4013’s active-high RESET. CD4076 has additional enable/control inputs; follow its truth table. For 4051/4052/4053 at 0–5 V, use VEE=GND and valid inhibit/select levels; their switches are bidirectional, but the AND/OR replacement exercises reproduce digital selection in one direction only. DM9368 has TTL input loading and integrated segment current sources: O01 includes HCT buffers for all twelve decoder inputs as well as the LED observation bar. Rewire to the DM9368 pinout; do not plug it into a 4511 socket. Its segment current and display rating must be checked with the actual legacy batch.

Counting method

Every exercise has a complete BOM, including reusable power, board and extractor. M–F estimates count three leads per separate button, nine per eight-channel LED bar, three per clock module and four per traffic-light module. M–M allowances are design estimates for power links, unused-input ties, fixed binary input patches, chip interconnects and support circuitry; they have not been counted from built layouts. Budgets range from 12 leads for the initial power exercise to 190 for the discrete serial adder. The kit takes each item’s maximum across its covered exercises, adds 25% to each jumper type, then rounds to whole 40-lead packs. Buttons round to packs of five. Decoupling capacitors get 20% spare, rounded up.

This reuse rule covers sequential lessons. It does not provide enough parts to leave all projects assembled or to distribute simultaneous projects across a class. For N parallel learners or pairs, multiply a selected complete kit by N. The comprehensive kit contains separate alternatives so a teacher can compare them by rebuilding; side-by-side duplication is included only where an exercise explicitly needs it.

Cost basis

All amounts are USD. LSSK column J is the sole source price baseline. It mixes low-quantity, bulk and marketplace lot assumptions; Jameco Q500 figures in column O are not silently substituted. Per-piece values for buttons and jumper packs are normalized, then kit quantities are rounded back to full packs. This estimates allocated kit cost from a batch purchase, not the checkout bill for one retail customer purchasing every listing separately. No current stock or price is claimed.

The five CSVs have component, allowance and summary record types. Sum only component rows for materials; summary rows repeat totals for convenience. All line costs are rounded to cents before summation. Loaded cost = materials + 15% inbound allowance + 5% replacement allowance + packaging + labor at $20/hour. Retail targets 40% contribution after a 3% payment fee, rounded up to $5. Labor and all percentage allowances are planning assumptions, not supplier quotations. Sensitivity ranges change component prices −25% / +50%; unusually scarce parts may vary more.

Each kit excludes a shared USB source, multimeter, teacher’s scope/logic analyzer, customer delivery and tax. The one-time curriculum and circuit-development effort is not amortized. Suggested prices should be revisited after a pilot procurement and timed assembly batch.

Validation and its limits

Automated checks compare CSV line items and totals with the generated HTML, prove that each kit covers every listed exercise quantity, check local links and image files, exhaust the arbiter’s Boolean behavior, and compare every offered FSM pattern against a suffix-recognition oracle. The actual JavaScript passed interaction checks using a minimal DOM stub in V8: 28 patterns, 57,316 streams and 516,152 sampled bits. Chromium could not start because the workspace sandbox blocked its socket/shutdown operations, so browser rendering and print layout remain unverified. These checks do not establish circuit timing, supplier authenticity, electrical loading or measured jumper consumption. The advanced FIFO and serial designs are explicitly marked for bench verification.
