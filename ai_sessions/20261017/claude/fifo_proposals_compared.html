Two 40105 FIFO proposals, compared

On 16 October you asked Codex and me for the same thing: a breadboard functional equivalent of flip_flop_fifo_empty_full_optimized_and_debug_2 built around the 40105. This page compares Codex's fifo_40105_proposal.html (its 4 × 16 version, saved 17 October at 23:41) with my fifo_sync_proposal.html (variant B of BB8 and P3).

I wrote one of the two proposals. To keep this fair, I re-ran every number below today, including Codex's own testbench. I also copied Codex's netlist pin for pin into my chip-level testbench, changing only D3, which is tied low because my test has three data buttons. Wherever Codex is ahead, the page says so.

Verdict

  • The circuits are almost the same. We reached the same core independently. The only behavioural difference is one gate: mine lets pop through only when the FIFO is not empty, as lab_top does with pop = ~empty & key[0]. Without that gate, pressing push and pop together on an empty FIFO starts a race between a 4069 inverter and the 40105's ripple-through, and the datasheets don't say who wins.
  • Codex ahead on build documents: a logic drawing, every chip pin, a one-board layout, a 50-wire schedule checked for shorts and missing connections, a timing diagram of your six-cycle scenario, and a Russian edition. Its circuit also uses one chip fewer.
  • Mine ahead on scope and checking: the FIFO adder (P3), a priced kit, and a simulation of every chip with timing corners, contact bounce, illegal requests and deliberate faults.
  • Best of both: Codex's four chips plus my pop gating fit with no extra chip. The gating uses Codex's two spare inverters and the NOR gate that made empty. This hybrid passes all five corners of my test (see below).

The circuit both arrived at

Codex, 4 × 16

on clk ↑:  {D_hold, W, R} ← {write_data, push, pop}
           (40174, all six flip-flops)
SI    = NOR (clk, W̄)        4001; W̄ from a 4069
SO    = NOT NOR (clk, R̄)    4001, then a 4069
full  = NOT DIR             4001 as an inverter
empty = NOT DOR             4001 as an inverter

Mine, BB8 variant B

on clk ↑:  push_r ← push            (4013)
           pop_r  ← pop AND DOR     (4082, 4013)
           D2..D0 ← data buttons    (40174)
SI    = NOR (clk, Q̄ of push_r)      4001
SO    = NOT NOR (clk, Q̄ of pop_r)   4001, third NOR
LEDs  = DOR (not empty), DIR (not full)

Both proposals argue each of these points the same way:

  • The clock drives the NOR input directly, so at the rising edge the clock masks the strobe before the newly captured bit can change. Both reject an inverted clock ANDed with the request, for the same glitch reason.
  • A 40174 captures the data at the rising edge, so each word is what the inputs showed at that edge.
  • A push into a full FIFO on the same edge as a pop is taken, because SI is still high when the place frees up. Both cite TI's functional description for this.
  • The request flip-flops are cleared by the same reset as MR, so SI stays low during a reset.
  • Depth is 16, and the RTL is instantiated at width 4 and depth 16 for the comparison.

Both datasheet figures are correct. At 4.5 V, TI gives a 500 ns maximum ripple time (SO to DIR) at 25 °C, which I quoted as 0.5 µs, and 750 ns over −55 to 125 °C, which Codex quoted.

The one real difference: push and pop on an empty FIFO

When the FIFO is empty and push and pop arrive on the same edge, SI rises and SO falls in the same low half-period. In Codex's circuit, SO falls one 4069 delay after SI rises. The new word then ripples through 16 places to the output:

  • If the word reaches the output (DOR high) before SO falls, the 40105 pops it at once, and the word is lost.
  • If SO falls first, the 40105 ignores it, and the word stays.

TI gives only a maximum ripple time (SI to DOR, 400 ns at 4.5 V and 25 °C), so the datasheets don't decide the race. On a breadboard with buttons, a student will press push and pop together on an empty FIFO, so this case can't be left to "the caller must obey the rules".

Best of both: four chips with the empty-pop gate

Codex's 4 × 16 circuit leaves two 4069 inverters unused. Making empty with an inverter instead of a NOR frees a NOR, and the three gates together make pop AND DOR:

U4.E  (11 → 10)     empty  = NOT DOR              was: input tied to GND
U4.F  (13 → 12)     pop_n  = NOT pop              was: input tied to GND
U3.D  (12, 13 → 11) pop_ok = NOR (pop_n, empty)   was: empty = NOR (DOR, DOR)
U2 pin 14 (D6)      pop_ok instead of pop

That uses all six inverters and all four NOR gates, and keeps empty and full in RTL polarity. In my chip-level testbench the hybrid has 0 mismatches in all five corners, including the slow-glue, fast-FIFO corner where Codex's circuit fails. It is simulated only: neither drawn nor laid out.

What each could take from the other

For Codex's proposal

  • The empty-pop gate above, at no chip cost.
  • The FIFO adder: its three-FIFO kit has no flip-flops for the clocked circuit.
  • Kit lists that match the proposal's chips.
  • Tests with illegal requests, presses at any moment and deliberate faults.
  • A simulation of the netlist it draws, not a separate behavioural model.

For mine

  • A layout of variant B, with a hole-by-hole schedule and a static wiring check like Codex's.
  • Push and pop in the 40174's spare flip-flops (the hybrid), saving a chip.
  • empty and full in RTL polarity.
  • A Russian edition and a timing diagram of your six-cycle scenario.
  • A testbench that also compiles with Icarus 14.

How this was checked

Every result on this page comes from one script, which runs in about a minute:

fifo_compare/run.sh
  • Codex's testbench: its own testbench (research/fifo_40105_proposal/testbench.sv and proposal_model.sv) with its three delay orders. All three pass, as Codex reports.
  • Push and pop on an empty FIFO: tb_empty.sv runs that case on Codex's model.
  • Six-cycle scenario: tb_six.sv runs it on my netlist, Codex's model and the RTL.
  • Chip-level testbench: my testbench from fifo_sync_proposal.sv, with the BB8 netlist swapped for codex_bb8.sv (Codex's four chips, plus a CD4069UB model at 0.44 × the 4001 delay) or hybrid_bb8.sv.
