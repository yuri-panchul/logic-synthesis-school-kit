Proposal: breadboard FIFOs that behave like the RTL FIFO

In the FPGA lab, flip_flop_fifo_empty_full_optimized_and_debug_2 samples push and pop at every rising clock edge, so holding push for three clock cycles pushes three words. The breadboard FIFO in the current build guide pushes one word per press, however long you hold the button. This proposal changes the glue logic around the 40105 so that exercises BB8 and P3 become functional equivalents of the RTL FIFO (depth 16, width 4). The build guides are unchanged until you decide; FIFO kit B lists the parts for this circuit.

1. What differs today

The RTL FIFO

At every rising edge of clk: if push is 1, write_data goes in; if pop is 1, the head goes out. read_data, empty and full show the state. Holding push for n clock cycles pushes n words. The lab's lab_top uses push = ~full & key[1] and pop = ~empty & key[0] on a slow clock.

The breadboard now

A 4013 flip-flop samples the push button at the rising edge and drives SI. The 40105 loads a word on a rising edge of SI, and SI must go low before the next word. While the button is held, SI stays high, so a press gives one word: holding it for three clock periods still pushes one. Pop works the same way on SO. In P3, the transfer flip-flop moves a pair at most every second clock.

2. The idea: sample at the rising edge, act in the low half

The 40105 is an asynchronous FIFO. It reacts to edges on SI and SO, not to a clock. To make it synchronous, every input of the RTL is sampled at the rising edge into a flip-flop, as the always_ff blocks do. Every sampled 1 then becomes exactly one pulse on SI or SO during the low half of that clock period:

SI = NOR (clk, Q̄push)    high from the falling edge to the next rising edge, if push was 1 at the rising edge
SO = NOT NOR (clk, Q̄pop)  falls at the falling edge (the 40105 drops a word on a falling SO), if pop was 1

The 40105 has finished long before the next rising edge: the datasheet gives at most 0.5 µs to ripple a word through all 16 places (4.5 V), and at 2 Hz the low half lasts 250 ms. So at every rising edge, Q0–Q3, DOR and DIR of the 40105 equal read_data, ~empty and ~full of the RTL for the same inputs. The one visible difference is that the breadboard's LEDs change at the falling edge, half a period after the RTL's outputs.

Why a NOR with the clock itself, not an AND with an inverted clock. When the clock rises, the clock input alone forces the NOR output low, before Q̄ can change (Q̄ changes about 150 ns later, through the flip-flop). When the clock falls, Q̄ has been stable for half a period. So SI cannot glitch, whatever the delays of the chips. With (NOT clk) AND Q, a glitch on SI would depend on the inverter being faster than the flip-flop. In the models it always is, which is why I could not show that fault failing, but the NOR form does not rely on it.

3. Details that make it exact

The data register (40174)

The 40105 takes D0–D3 at the rising edge of SI, which now comes at the falling clock edge. The 40174 samples the switches at the rising edge, together with push, so each word is what the switches showed at that rising edge, as in the RTL. Without it, a held value still works (5 5 5), but switches changed 1 → 2 → 3 right after the rising edges store 2 3 3 (fault 2 below).

Pop only when not empty

Suppose the FIFO is empty and push and pop come on the same edge. The new word ripples to the output in a few hundred nanoseconds. If SO falls after it arrives, the 40105 pops the word that was just pushed. Whether that happens depends on which is faster, the gates or the FIFO. In the simulation it happens in the "slow glue, fast FIFO" corner (fault 1). Sampling pop AND DOR at the rising edge rules it out. P3 uses the second gate of its 4082 for this, so it costs P3 nothing.

Push into a full FIFO

No gate is needed. Without a pop on that edge, the 40105 ignores SI because DIR is low. With a pop on the same edge, SI is still high when the place frees up, and the 40105 takes the word. The datasheet says: "If during unloading SI is HIGH (FIFO is full) data on the data input of the FIFO is entered in the first location." That is the push-with-pop behaviour that your FIFO supports and that its tb.sv tests with allow_push_when_full_with_pop = 1. lab_top uses the stricter push = ~full & key[1]. Matching that exactly needs DIR ANDed into the push flip-flop's D: the spare 4082 gate does this in BB8, but P3 has no spare AND.

Reset

The push, pop and go flip-flops are reset together with the FIFOs, so SI is low while MR is high. That matters because the datasheet says: "If the shift-in flag (SI) is HIGH during the master reset pulse, data present at the input (D0 to D3) are immediately moved into the first location upon completion of the reset process." With the go flip-flop not reset, a reset during a transfer leaves a stray word in C (fault 6).

4. P3: one pair per clock, as in the RTL

With synchronous FIFOs the transfer logic is the RTL expression itself: go = ~empty_A & ~empty_B & ~full_C, that is DORA·DORB·DIRC on the 4082. The go flip-flop samples it at the rising edge. In the low half that follows, SI of C rises, and one gate delay later SO of A and B fall (SOA,B = NOT SIC). So C takes the sum before A and B let go of the pair, and C's 25 ns data hold time is met by construction. While A and B have data and C has room, a pair moves on every clock. This replaces the transfer flip-flop t, whose two-clock handshake (go = rA·rB·fC·t̄) let at most one pair through every second clock.

7. Alternatives considered

Act at the rising edge, sample at the falling edge

Clock the flip-flops with an inverted clock and use SI = clk AND Q and SO = NAND (clk, Q). The 40105 then takes the switches at the rising edge itself, so it needs no 40174, and the LEDs change at the rising edge as on the FPGA. But push and pop are read half a period before the edge they act on: a press shortly before a rising edge lands one clock later. A synchronous data source would also have to hold its data for the SI gate delay after the edge, which acts as clock skew. It saves the 40174 and about 13 jumpers.

SI = clock AND push, without a flip-flop

This is the pulse in the Habr article. Contact bounce and a press in the middle of a clock-high phase make extra or shortened pulses (fault 3: caught everywhere).

Keep the current circuit

One word per press is simpler to wire, but it is not the RTL's behaviour, and P3 then needs the two-clock transfer handshake.

8. Decisions for you

  1. The 40174 data register: keep it for exact equivalence (recommended), or drop it to save a chip and about 13 jumpers. Without it, the switches must not change in the first half period after a rising edge while push is held.
  2. Push into a full FIFO on the same edge as a pop: accept it as your FIFO allows (no extra gate, recommended), or reject it like lab_top (one AND per FIFO: free in BB8, an extra chip in P3).
  3. Go ahead: re-route and regenerate the guides, the SV model and the kits as listed in section 6.
