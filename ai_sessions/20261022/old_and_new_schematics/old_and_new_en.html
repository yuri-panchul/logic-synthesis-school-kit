These are reconstructions of the two texts. Panel B replaces the XOR experiment; it is not wired to panel A. References such as U1 and C1 restart in each panel. The alternative AND/OR/inverter substitutions and optional /Q observation are not additional assembled circuits here.

One indicator channel is shown. The old guide’s list provisions eight 2.2 kΩ resistors for the full observer, but its first-channel instructions use only R1; the seven unused buffer inputs are grounded and their outputs are unconnected. LED1 means the specified current-limited 5 V LED, or channel 0 of the passive common-cathode 5 V LED bar. Its existing internal current limiting is part of that component.

1. Generic logic

XOR, a positive-edge D flip-flop, asynchronous RESET, the old buffer and two Schmitt inverters are shown without chip-specific pin numbers. The old power capacitors are shown separately below the logic.

2. Connections between chips

Signal wires carry actual pin numbers. The lists under each circuit specify power, every grounded unused input and every unconnected output. Pin locations in this drawing are schematic, not a physical package view.

3. Placement and wiring on a breadboard

Each panel is a complete separate layout with shared five-hole strips, explicit rail bridges and component pins. The connection tables below give exact holes. Open SVG for inspection at any zoom.

  • The DIP notch faces LEFT. Pin 1 is the lower-left pin on row f; the highest-numbered pin is directly above it on row e. Each chip straddles the trench.
  • a–e at one column form one contact; f–j form another. The trench separates them. TP/BP are top/bottom positive rails; TG/BG are top/bottom ground rails. Rail numbering belongs to this drawing.
  • The illustrated rail has 50 holes and a break between 25 and 26. Four short bridges join the halves; two long jumpers connect top and bottom rails. Match these to the continuity of the actual board.
  • PWR shows the regulated 5 V output connection. Its input path is USB 5 V → 9 V boost cable → breadboard regulator set to 5 V. Only the regulated output feeds the logic.
  • External modules are drawn by terminal names, not a guaranteed connector pin order. Use the actual VCC, GND and OUT markings. Lines connect at endpoint dots; a crossing without a dot is not a connection.
  • Power off while rewiring. Build either A or B, connect rails and fixed input levels, then signals, and check against the hole table before power-on.

Checks, scope and references

All four layouts passed a breadboard connectivity check: no shared occupied hole, no short between distinct nets, every required net connected across strips and rail bridges, and every unused output isolated. Active pin paths and chip orientation were also checked. Both source guides remain unchanged. This is a drawing/netlist verification; no physical build or analog timing/current measurement was performed.
